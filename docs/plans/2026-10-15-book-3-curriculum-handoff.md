## 2. Purpose and level

Book 1, *Fondations*, introduces A1–B1 grammar in 25 modules. Book 2,
*En situation*, applies it in 25 modules of short dialogues. Book 3 takes the
learner from that base toward B2 through connected text, precise expression,
and vocabulary in context. Completion alone does not certify a CEFR level.

Book 2 already includes basic recounting, comparison, advice, opinion,
disagreement, reasons, and realistic `si` clauses. Book 3 deepens these skills
across multiple sentences and viewpoints. It does not restart their basic
lessons. The [CEFR Companion Volume](https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2020/16809ea0d4)
is a level reference, not a fixed French grammar syllabus.

## 3. Decisions from the user

- Every drill shows its French source text. Audio can accompany visible text,
  but no drill tests listening without text.
- There is **no top hint or separate scene-setting cue**. In a dialogue drill,
  the messages provide the context. A short task label or question is fine.
- Each card has one clean, compact task and a short answer. Typed answers are
  short where typing fits the task.
- Use multiple formats, not one dialogue format throughout the book. The
  proposed set is **dialogue, microtext, rewrite, and word in context**.
- Teach grammar and vocabulary together in each module. Reuse new expressions
  in later modules, not only in the module that introduces them.
- Facts needed for the answer, including person, referent, and register, must
  appear in the visible source or the short task. Do not add a hidden hint.
- Start with people, settings, and core words familiar from Book 2. Introduce
  one useful expression in a clear context, reuse it, and only then ask the
  learner to produce it. Do not jump between several unfamiliar settings in a
  short pilot.
- Increase difficulty gradually within each module and across the book. Raise
  one demand at a time: source length, inference, grammar, vocabulary, or answer
  freedom.
- In the Test book layout, the French source sits directly on the blue block.
  Dialogue speaker labels remain visible; there are no white message boxes.
  Keep the text inset from the rounded corners.
- Teach harder vocabulary explicitly. The first vocabulary drill for a harder
  word offers English meanings; later cards reuse the French expression. Do
  not gloss every familiar word or increase difficulty by adding untaught words.

These are working requirements. The module titles and lexical examples below
are the current curriculum proposal; pilot feedback can refine them.

## 4. Pilot formats

| Format | Visible source | One compact action |
|---|---|---|
| Dialogue | A few messages, with optional audio and visible text | Reply, identify a meaning, or choose a fitting expression |
| Microtext | A short message, notice, review, or paragraph | Answer one question or state one inference |
| Rewrite | A visible sentence or pair of sentences | Change time, reference, relation, or tone |
| Word in context | A phrase in a visible sentence | Select or produce the meaning or fitting phrase |

The Test book draft has 10 cards in module 01, four in module 02, and six in
each later module. These counts are review samples, not production quotas.
Pick the format that checks the module outcome and keep one task per card.
Exact text lengths, production grading, and further vocabulary support remain
open.

## 5. Twenty-module map

### Arc 1 — Raconter avec précision

| # | Module | Outcome | Grammar and vocabulary in use | Source idea |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Ce qui s'est passé | Separate the setting from an event in an account. | *Imparfait* vs *passé composé*; *une panne, s'interrompre, au moment où*. | Léa, Théo, and Inès at a concert. |
| 02 | Ce qui s'était déjà passé | State which action happened before the main event. | *Plus-que-parfait* with familiar *déjà* and *avant*; defer new lexical demands while this tense is introduced. | Two short messages about the same familiar event. |
| 03 | D'après elle… | Report a person's account without stating it as fact. | Reported speech; start with *elle dit que* and *selon elle* before stronger reporting verbs. | Two familiar characters describe one event. |
| 04 | Deux versions | Reconstruct a cautious timeline from two accounts. | Sequence and simultaneity; *vérifier, peut-être, en fait* in context. | Two brief accounts with one visible difference. |

### Arc 2 — Expliquer et relier

| # | Module | Outcome | Grammar and vocabulary in use | Source idea |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 05 | De qui, de quoi ? | Identify the exact person or thing that a text refers to. | *Dont, à qui, auquel, à laquelle*; *faire référence à, parler de*. | A caption and a message about an exhibit. |
| 06 | Ce qui compte | Summarize a need or priority without repeating its wording. | *Ce qui, ce que, ce dont*; *tenir à, manquer de, mettre en avant*. | Several short survey responses. |
| 07 | Transmettre sans répéter | State who sent what to whom without losing the referent. | Combined object pronouns; *transmettre, faire parvenir, signaler*. | A short group thread about a shared project. |
| 08 | Pourquoi le plan a changé | Explain a cause, consequence, and decision. | *Parce que, puisque, donc, si bien que*; *faute de, reporter, entraîner*. | A brief update about a changed event. |

### Arc 3 — Choisir et défendre

| # | Module | Outcome | Grammar and vocabulary in use | Source idea |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 09 | Peser les options | Compare measurable trade-offs and justify a choice. | Quantity comparison and contrast; *un délai, des frais, fiable, ça vaut le coup*. | Two options with visible prices and time. |
| 10 | Poser ses conditions | State the condition for an agreement. | *Si* vs *à condition que* and *pourvu que*; *confirmer, sous réserve de*. | A message about plans that depend on availability. |
| 11 | Revenir sur un choix | Explain a missed chance or regret. | *Si* + *plus-que-parfait* → conditional past; *au lieu de, éviter, regretter*. | A short account of a decision and its result. |
| 12 | Concéder, puis défendre | Acknowledge a drawback and support a conclusion. | *Même si, bien que, malgré*; *pourtant, néanmoins, convaincant*. | Two short reviews of the same proposal. |

### Arc 4 — Réagir aux autres

| # | Module | Outcome | Grammar and vocabulary in use | Source idea |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | Demander avec tact | Make a precise request in a formal message. | Indirect questions and polite conditional; *pourriez-vous préciser, une démarche, un délai*. | A brief email exchange. |
| 14 | Réagir à une nouvelle | Express relief, surprise, or disappointment about an outcome. | Emotion with present or past subjunctive; *soulagée, étonnée, aboutir*. | A message that announces a result. |
| 15 | Distinguer fait et doute | Pass on uncertain information responsibly. | Asserted fact with indicative vs doubt with subjunctive; *une rumeur, confirmer, supposer*. | A message with an unconfirmed claim. |
| 16 | Négocier un désaccord | Acknowledge a constraint and propose a compromise. | *Je vois que* vs *je préférerais que*; *une échéance, provisoire, s'engager à*. | A short exchange about conflicting plans. |

### Arc 5 — Lire entre les lignes

| # | Module | Outcome | Grammar and vocabulary in use | Source idea |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 17 | Qui dit quoi ? | Separate an event from people's claims about it. | Passive and reported speech; *un témoignage, une enquête, être mis en cause*. | Two short reports of one event. |
| 18 | Qui a fait faire quoi ? | Identify who arranged an action and who did it. | *Faire / se faire + infinitive*; *faire réparer, prendre en charge*. | A repair message or service report. |
| 19 | Deux avis, une nuance | Compare viewpoints and detect reservation or implied disagreement. | Reference across sentences and concession; *en revanche, nuancer, remettre en question*. | Two reviews or comments. |
| 20 | Faire la synthèse | Draw a brief supported conclusion from two sources. | Reuse tense, reference, condition, concession, and register. Reuse earlier vocabulary. | Two visible texts about one issue. |

The source genres above are examples. Vary subjects across personal events,
culture, neighborhood life, work, travel, and public discussion. Avoid a book
made mainly of office and administrative problems. Module 20 adds no new
headline grammar.

### Difficulty ladder

Increase difficulty a little at a time across the book. The module map sets the
skill progression; it is not permission to introduce a fresh grammar pattern,
unfamiliar setting, long text, and several new words on the same card.
The expressions in the map are candidates to stage and recycle, not a list to
teach all at once in each module.

| Modules | Reading and response progression |
|---|---|
| 01–04 | Familiar Book 2 people, places, and words. One or two short messages. Identify a stated event, order, or speaker; produce a short controlled answer. |
| 05–08 | Two or three connected sentences. Track a referent or a cause across sentences; restate a point in familiar words. |
| 09–12 | Short choices or viewpoints with visible evidence. Compare, give one reason, then add a condition or concession. |
| 13–16 | Brief exchanges with a change in tone, certainty, or register. Choose or write a fitting response using the information shown. |
| 17–20 | Two short sources about one issue. Separate fact from claim, notice a reservation, and finish with a brief supported synthesis. |

Within a module, begin with recognition in an easy source, then ask for one
small application, then transfer the same expression to a nearby context.
Recycle earlier expressions throughout. Teach a new word from a visible clue
before asking the learner to produce it. Review the first modules in the Test
book app before setting exact text lengths or new-word counts.
